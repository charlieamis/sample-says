How this works (and what it does not mean)
  • The “survey” is one model, sampled 100 times. We show a base model the top of a survey-results page and let it write one respondent's answer, 100 times, at temperature 1. Those are 100 draws from one probability distribution, not 100 independent people.
  • Clustering: answers that mean the same thing are grouped (“Dog”, “a dog”, “dogs” → Dog). Only answers given by at least two samples go on the board, up to 8.
  • Judging: a guess that matches a group reveals it. If it doesn't, we ask the model directly how likely it thought your exact answer was (its log-probability, ending with a line break so “dog” doesn't get credit for “doghouse”).
  • Counts are noisy. 100 samples is a small poll; run the same question twice and the board will shift a little. The “exact %” is the model's own probability, without sampling noise.
  • A base model isn't answering as itself; it's predicting what text comes next on a page like this. That makes it a mirror of how people write, which is why it often looks like a crowd.
